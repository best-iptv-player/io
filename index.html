GUIDE

What IPTV actually is

IPTV just means "television delivered over an internet connection instead of satellite dish or cable coax." That's it. The confusing part is that the term covers both fully licensed services and unlicensed ones — here's how to tell them apart.

The basics Legit vs. not Glossary Choosing a service

The basics

Traditional TV sends every channel down a dedicated line or over the air, whether you're watching it or not. IPTV instead delivers a specific stream to your device over standard internet infrastructure, the same way a video call or a downloaded file gets to you. That's why an IPTV setup can run on a phone, a laptop, a smart TV app, or a set-top box interchangeably — it's just data.

This delivery method itself is neutral technology. Major broadcasters, telecom companies, and household-name streaming services all use IPTV or IPTV-adjacent delivery under the hood. YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, and Fubo are all, technically, IPTV services with proper content licenses.

Legit services vs. unlicensed reselling

!

The word "IPTV" gets used as a euphemism for illegal reselling of copyrighted channels — usually marketed as "IPTV subscriptions" offering thousands of channels for a few dollars a month. These operations redistribute broadcasts without paying for the rights, which is copyright infringement, and they carry real risk for the person paying for them: no support if it stops working, exposure to malware-laden apps, payment fraud, and in some jurisdictions legal exposure for the subscriber too.

A simple filter: if a deal advertises "every channel and every sports package for $10/month," that price doesn't cover what broadcasters actually charge for those rights — so the service isn't paying for them. Licensed live-TV streaming (see the comparison below) runs $40–$90+ a month for a reason.

Terms you'll run into

M3U / M3U8
A plain-text playlist file format that lists channel names and their stream URLs. Any player that supports M3U can load a legitimately-provided playlist from a licensed service.
Xtream Codes API
A common backend that IPTV providers (legal or not) use to serve channel lists, EPG data, and video-on-demand to apps. It's a protocol, not a service in itself.
EPG (Electronic Program Guide)
The schedule data — what's on, when — usually delivered as an XMLTV file alongside the channel list.
HLS / MPEG-TS
The actual video streaming formats used to deliver the picture and sound to your player, regardless of who's providing the channel.
Catch-up TV / timeshift
The ability to rewind or replay a live channel's recent broadcast window, offered by some licensed providers as part of the subscription.

How to choose a service

  • Check that the provider is a named, identifiable company — not just a Telegram channel or a reseller with no website.
  • Compare its channel lineup and price against the licensed services below. If it's a fraction of the price for the same channels, ask why.
  • Look for a real refund policy, real customer support, and reviews outside of the provider's own site.
  • Remember that a player app (like Best IPTV Player) is separate from a content provider — a good player doesn't make an unlicensed subscription legitimate.
